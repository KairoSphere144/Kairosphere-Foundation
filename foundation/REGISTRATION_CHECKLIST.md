# Suriname Foundation — Registration and Verification Checklist

**Status:** Research checklist; current fees and filing practice remain unconfirmed.

## A. Legal requirements established from the Civil Code

- [x] Book 2, Title 2, Articles 50–58 governs the stichting.
- [x] Article 50: foundation is a legal person established by notarial deed, except where an officially prescribed standard model deed may be used; no members or shareholders; statutory purpose may not provide distributions to founders or governing-organ members, subject to narrow statutory exceptions.
- [x] Article 51: statutes must state the foundation's name (including “Stichting” or translation), purpose, method of appointment and dismissal of directors, statutory seat in Suriname, and destination of the liquidation surplus or method of determining it.
- [x] Article 51(2): statutory amendment is possible only where and to the extent the statutes allow it.
- [x] Article 4: where a notarial deed is required, it is in Dutch and includes the statutes and names/residences of the initial directors and other required officers.
- [x] Article 5: the notary is responsible for ensuring compliance and arranging prompt Trade Register filing and deposit of an authentic copy of the deed and attachments.
- [x] Articles 8–16: board duties, representation, continuity, administration, annual accounts and director responsibilities must be considered in the statutes and governance design.
- [x] Article 58: a foundation maintaining an enterprise that must be registered under the Trade Register Act is subject to the Annual Accounts Act.

Official sources:

## B. Confirm before paying or executing

- [ ] Ask KKF whether a foundation is currently filed in the Trade Register, the Foundations Register, or both; identify the legal basis and responsible filer for each.
- [ ] Ask the notary to confirm whether the Foundations Act and Foundations Register requirements remain in force after the new Civil Code and 2025 Trade Register changes, and whether any transitional rules apply.
- [ ] Ask whether a government-approved standard model deed for a stichting has been issued under Article 50(1), where to obtain it and whether it can be used for this proposed purpose and governance model.
- [ ] Ask notary to confirm minimum board size, founder/director eligibility, residence requirements and required identity/civil-status documents.
- [ ] Confirm acceptable evidence for statutory seat and actual office address.
- [ ] Obtain current written fee quotations: name check/reservation, deed/model deed, notarisation/legalisation, filing, extracts, annual contributions and any publication.
- [ ] Confirm the exact sequence and whether the notary submits filings electronically or physically.
- [ ] Obtain written tax guidance for donations, grants, training income, sponsorship and any fee-generating activities.
- [ ] Confirm bank account KYC documents, signatories, fees and minimum balances before choosing a bank.

## C. Documents to prepare privately

- [ ] Founder/director identity documents and civil-status details as requested by notary.
- [ ] Proposed names and purpose statement.
- [ ] Draft statutes and board appointment resolutions.
- [ ] Evidence for registered seat/address.
- [ ] Contact and representation details.
- [ ] Initial asset/endowment statement if required.
- [ ] Conflict-of-interest declarations.
- [ ] Initial programme and budget.

Do not place identity documents, residential addresses, signatures or financial identifiers in this public repository.

## D. Filing and post-registration evidence

- [ ] Executed deed or approved model deed evidence.
- [ ] Trade Register filing/registration confirmation, where applicable.
- [ ] Foundations Register filing/registration confirmation, where applicable.
- [ ] Current official extracts and registration numbers.
- [ ] Tax registration and written filing position.
- [ ] Board minutes, resolutions and signatory authority.
- [ ] Separate bank account and accounting records.
- [ ] Annual reporting calendar and document-retention system.

## E. Business-permit process

The Government's current page states that permit applications are submitted through KKF as front office, while ministry assessment is back office. Whether any proposed foundation activity requires a permit must be determined from the actual activity list and current law.

## F. Important distinction in older sources

The 2025 National Risk Assessment describes a separate Foundations Register and cites the 1968 Foundations Act. It was prepared around a legal transition and predates the Civil Code's commencement on 1 May 2025. Treat it as a useful indication of registry practice, not conclusive proof of the current post-transition filing route. Obtain confirmation from KKF and the notary.
